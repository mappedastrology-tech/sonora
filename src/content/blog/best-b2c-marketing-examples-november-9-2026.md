The best B2C marketing this week started with something people were already doing: quoting a comment, looking for their name in print, dodging Thanksgiving chores, reusing a tub. These seven campaigns ran or broke through in early November, and each one holds an idea another brand can use.

## Smoothie King put a celebrity's passing comment on its menu

![A still from Smoothie King's launch video announcing Connor's Paid Me No Mind Smoothie](/images/blog/b2c-nov-9/smoothie-king.jpg)

Jennifer Lawrence left an Instagram comment for *Dancing With the Stars* comedian Connor Wood: "I saw you on 8th avenue drinking a smoothie and you paid me no mind." She didn't name a brand, so the joke was available to any smoothie chain paying attention.

Smoothie King got there first. Eight days after [Wood shared the comment](https://entertainmentnow.com/dancing-with-the-stars/connor-wood-reveals-update-after-jennifer-lawrences-viral-comment/), it launched [Connor's Paid Me No Mind Smoothie](https://roastbrief.us/connor-wood-and-smoothie-king-have-teamed-up-to-officially-drop-connors-paid-me-no-mind-smoothie/) with Wood, in stores only, at [Smoothie King locations nationwide](https://entertainmentnow.com/dancing-with-the-stars/connor-wood-viral-smoothie-king-jennifer-lawrence/).

**What to steal:** watch for viral moments that mention your category without mentioning a competitor. A generic noun in a popular line belongs to whoever moves first.

## John Lewis's most criticized Christmas ad tested as its best since 2019

![A grandmother greets her family at the front door in John Lewis's Christmas ad](/images/blog/b2c-nov-9/john-lewis.jpg)

In "Gloria and Pickles," a three-minute film from Saatchi & Saatchi, a grandmother named Gloria has given up and started putting cash in her grandchildren's Christmas cards. A talking toy dog tells her she can do better. The two set off across London, and on Christmas morning the children find her home office rebuilt as a "Gran Cave" full of disco balls and lights.

The reaction in the UK press was brutal. The Independent's Katie Rosseinsky called it tone deaf, columnist Sarah Vine called the grandchildren "spoilt, entitled, rude and overindulged," some viewers said Gloria's affluent London home was out of touch with the cost of living, and Piers Morgan called it possibly the worst ad he had seen.

Then come the test results, [collected by DesignRush](https://news.designrush.com/john-lewis-saatchi-2026-christmas-ad-backlash). The ad scored 5.6 stars on System1, whose scale tops out at 5.9, making it John Lewis' strongest Christmas ad since 2019. Only 15% of tested viewers recalled the children as rude. The picture isn't perfectly rosy: Kantar's head of creative excellence said it "lacks the emotive punch of previous years," and in OnePulse's polling 36% said it made them more likely to shop at John Lewis while 58% said it made no difference.

**What to steal:** before you call a campaign a failure, compare the comment section with a sample of people who didn't choose to comment.

## Nike turned last year's punchline into this year's ad

![A New York billboard for Nike reading Fancy meeting 60,229 of you here](/images/blog/b2c-nov-9/nike.jpg)

Nike has [advertised in The New York Times after the NYC Marathon for the past three years](https://www.marketingbrew.com/stories/nikes-nyc-marathon-new-york-times). Last year it took over six pages, opening with the line "I ran 26.2 miles and all I got was my name in the paper."

This year the Times' advertising team pitched the literal version. The paper has printed a partial list of finishers since 1994, and for the first time it printed all of them, half in Monday's edition and half in Tuesday's, in special sections Nike sponsored. Nike also put up billboards across the city and made copies available in some of its New York stores.

**What to steal:** give people proof they did the thing. A finisher will keep a newspaper with their name in it long after they've forgotten a banner ad.

## Butterball built a gadget that does the asking

![The Butterball Buddy, a blue handheld gadget displaying a chore on its screen](/images/blog/b2c-nov-9/butterball.jpg)

According to Butterball's 2026 Togetherness Report, 85% of Thanksgiving hosts would prefer guests offer to help, and only 38% actually ask. The [Butterball Buddy](https://www.dexerto.com/food/butterball-unveils-free-thanksgiving-gadget-that-makes-guests-do-chores-3423983/) closes that gap. Guests pass the free, turkey-shaped gadget around the room until it chimes and vibrates, and whoever is holding it gets a task. Limited batches drop on November 2, 9 and 16.

**What to steal:** look for the awkward moment around your product. Butterball's turkey has nothing to do with who clears the table, and that social problem is what earned the coverage.

## Apotek Hjärtat made supplement waste impossible to forget

![A bottle of Vitamin P(ee) gummies with yellow drop-shaped gummies on a green background](/images/blog/b2c-nov-9/vitamin-pee.jpg)

Sweden's largest pharmacy chain [developed "Vitamin P(ee)"](https://roastbrief.us/swedish-pharmacy-turns-human-pee-into-vitamins-demonstrating-the-consequences-of-supplement-overuse/), gummies made from vitamin C recovered from human urine, working with a researcher at Uppsala University. The point is that water-soluble vitamins your body doesn't need pass straight through, so people overusing supplements are flushing money down the drain.

**What to steal:** when your message is a warning people have tuned out, make the consequence into an object. A bottle of pee gummies gets sent to the group chat in a way a PSA about vitamin intake never will.

## Levi's, e.l.f. and Chewy are selling visible effort

![Levi's claymation yeti bursting through a log cabin](/images/blog/b2c-nov-9/levis.jpg)

![Chewy's stop-motion winter landscape with a hilltop Chewy castle](/images/blog/b2c-nov-9/chewy.jpg)

Three brands went handmade for the holidays. [Levi's "Let's Get Fuzzy"](https://musebyclios.com/fashion-and-beauty/a-claymation-yeti-stomps-through-levis-holiday-campaign/), made with Blinkink, took four hours of filming for each second of animation. [e.l.f.'s animated spot](https://musebyclios.com/film-tv/alan-cumming-narrates-e-l-f-s-animated-holiday-spot/) is narrated by Alan Cumming. [Chewy blends stop-motion with live action](https://musebyclios.com/advertising/chewy-joins-the-stop-motion-avalanche/), and its chief brand officer, Melissa Bell, confirmed there were "no AI-generated visuals used in the production."

**What to steal:** when generated video fills the feed, the labor becomes part of the message. If your team made something the hard way, say so.

## Lala designed its tub for its second life

![Stacked Lala crema tubs, the top one labelled Tinga in the write-in space](/images/blog/b2c-nov-9/lala.jpg)

Anyone who has opened a crema tub in a Mexican household knows it might be holding leftovers instead. Lala's tubs are opaque, so you can't tell without opening the lid. The dairy brand [added a space on the packaging](https://thedieline.com/whats-in-that-reused-lala-crema-tub-mexican-households-no-longer-have-to-guess/) for people to write in what's actually inside.

**What to steal:** find out what customers do with your packaging after the product is gone, then design for that.

## All seven campaigns started by watching people

A comment on Instagram, a marathon tradition, a host too polite to ask for help, a tub in the fridge: each of these was already part of someone's life before a brand showed up.
